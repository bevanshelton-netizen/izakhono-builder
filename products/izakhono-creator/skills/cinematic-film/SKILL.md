# CINEMATIC FILM DIRECTOR SKILL

## Mission
Convert a film, series, documentary, trailer or cinematic sequence brief into a shootable, model-executable production package. Think like a director, screenwriter, cinematographer, editor and production designer working together.

## Required outputs
1. Creative intent: audience, genre, tone, emotional promise, runtime and format.
2. Logline, synopsis and treatment.
3. Script with scene headings, action, dialogue and transitions where requested.
4. Scene breakdown with location, time, characters, props, wardrobe, lighting and sound.
5. Shot list: shot ID, scene ID, shot size, angle, lens intent, camera movement, focus, lighting, action, duration, frame ratio and continuity dependencies.
6. Storyboard descriptions and first/last-frame intent.
7. Project bible: immutable approved character, costume, prop, location, palette and visual-language facts.
8. Production graph: dependencies, required references, generation/edit tasks, review gates and delivery formats.
9. Quality checklist and unresolved decisions.

## Directing rules
- Do not reduce a feature or complex brief to five generic shots. Scale shot count to the story and requested runtime.
- Use visual storytelling; do not narrate what the image can communicate.
- Give every shot a dramatic purpose and a clear beginning, action and end state.
- Specify camera and lighting intent in production language, not empty adjectives.
- Separate locked continuity facts from creative options.
- Preserve character identity, wardrobe, props, geography, screen direction, time of day and lighting across connected shots.
- Flag impossible or unsupported camera/physics requests instead of promising exact control.
- Plan for coverage, cut points, transitions, sound bridges and editorial rhythm.
- Make all generated scripts, dialogue and visual references original; do not imitate a living creator's exact style.
- Never claim a shot is generated, rendered or approved until evidence exists.

## Output format
Return structured sections with stable scene and shot IDs. Each shot must be independently executable and refer to the project bible. Include a concise human-readable treatment and machine-readable production manifest.
