# IZAKHONO GLOBAL / I-CONNECT — Partner Engagement Playbook

**Status:** Internal business-development plan; no partnership is implied  
**Market:** South Africa  
**Approach:** Non-exclusive, evidence-led, provider-neutral.

## 1. Objective

Secure commercially viable wholesale, referral, reseller, integration, subcontracting or managed-service arrangements. IZAKHONO retains ownership of its software, customer experience, customer relationship, pricebook logic, billing orchestration and operational evidence.

## 2. Target counterparties

Approach relevant teams at:
- MTN Business — enterprise connectivity, SME distribution, API/integration and channel opportunities.
- Telkom Business — enterprise ICT, fibre-enabled services, channel and subcontracting opportunities.
- Openserve — wholesale fibre access and serviceability/ordering interfaces.
- BCX — enterprise integration, managed services and subcontracting opportunities.
- Other fibre operators, ISPs and MVNEs — diversify coverage and wholesale options.
- SIP/voice, SMS and messaging providers — only for scoped services and compliance-ready integration.

These are target counterparties, not current partners or evidence of service availability.

## 3. Engagement tracks

### Track A — wholesale connectivity
Request serviceability process, wholesale pricebook, installation lead-time definitions, SLA, fault escalation, order/cancel/status APIs, migration and settlement terms.

### Track B — channel/reseller
Request eligibility, territory, branding, customer ownership, commission, support allocation, training, customer consent and termination/portability terms.

### Track C — technology integration
Request API documentation, sandbox, authentication, rate limits, webhooks, test data, certification checklist, production credential process and incident contacts.

### Track D — enterprise subcontracting
Offer scoped software engineering, infrastructure assessment, migration, integration, security remediation and managed operations. Submit capability evidence and references only where verifiable.

### Track E — fibre/last-mile ecosystem
Request address-level serviceability, access products, wholesale rates, installation responsibilities, demarcation, SLA and escalation. Never promise service based on map coverage alone.

## 4. Partner qualification scorecard

Score each candidate from 1–5 and record evidence:
- commercial margin and wholesale price stability (20%);
- coverage and fulfilment reliability (15%);
- technical integration readiness (15%);
- SLA and escalation quality (15%);
- customer ownership and portability (10%);
- legal/regulatory responsibility allocation (10%);
- data protection/security maturity (10%);
- settlement, credit and reconciliation (5%).

A high score does not replace mandatory legal, regulatory, security or operational gates. A failed mandatory gate disqualifies production activation.

## 5. Required due diligence

Before signature, verify:
- legal entity and authorised signatory;
- licence/authorisation status and exact service scope;
- master services agreement, reseller/wholesale addendum and data-processing terms;
- customer ownership, branding and marketing approval;
- wholesale pricebook, taxes, price changes and pass-through charges;
- serviceability and coverage evidence;
- service levels, exclusions, maintenance and incident escalation;
- APIs, sandbox, security controls, webhook signing and certification;
- RICA/KYC allocation where relevant;
- lawful requests, retention and regulatory responsibility allocation;
- billing, settlement, refunds, disputes, credits and audit;
- support model, training and escalation;
- portability, termination, data export and transition assistance;
- liability, insurance and business continuity.

## 6. First meeting agenda

1. Introductions and business model.
2. IZAKHONO Infrastructure and I-CONNECT overview.
3. Customer segments and initial pilot.
4. Requested model: wholesale, reseller, referral, integration or subcontracting.
5. Product availability and geographic scope.
6. API and operational integration.
7. Compliance and responsibility allocation.
8. Commercial model and margin assumptions.
9. Pilot cohort, success measures and decision owners.
10. Next steps, owners and dates.

## 7. Pilot principles

- Start with a small, explicitly bounded cohort.
- Use real provider services only after contract, pricebook, credentials, certification and legal gates.
- Obtain customer consent and disclose underlying provider and service terms.
- Reconcile every order, charge, fulfilment and cancellation.
- Measure provisioning success, fulfilment time, faults, support cost, refunds, margin and customer satisfaction.
- Stop new orders if uncertain fulfilment, security incident, billing divergence or regulatory issue occurs.
