# FORTRESS — INSTITUTIONAL TARGET ACCOUNT STRATEGY

## 1. Objective

Convert FORTRESS from a technology programme into institutional pilots, paid deployments and recurring enterprise revenue, beginning in South Africa and expanding through regional payment and financial ecosystems.

The immediate objective is **not** to sell a generic cybersecurity product. It is to put a measurable financial-crime decision and intelligence layer in front of institutions already facing material fraud pressure.

## 2. Market signal

South Africa's 2025 reported digital banking crime losses reached R2.4 billion, with banking-app crime accounting for more than 70% of reported digital banking losses. SABRIC identifies collaboration, intelligence sharing and stronger fraud prevention as important responses to the evolving threat environment.

SARB's Payments Ecosystem Modernisation Programme explicitly includes an insights capability and fraud framework intended to turn payments data into actionable intelligence and proactively combat fraud, money laundering and terrorist financing. SARB's position paper also identifies centralised fraud and intelligence capability as important to safe, trustworthy digital payments.

**Strategic conclusion:** FORTRESS is entering a problem category that is already a stated institutional priority. The sales proposition should therefore be evidence-led: improve detection, reduce avoidable loss, control false positives, shorten decision latency and improve investigation productivity.

## 4. Account-selection score

Every prospect should be scored 1–5 against:

- Fraud pressure
- Transaction volume/value
- Digital/API maturity
- Executive urgency
- Ability to run a controlled pilot
- Potential recurring contract value
- Network-effect value
- Procurement accessibility
- Regulatory fit
- Reference value

Priority score = weighted sum, with fraud pressure, transaction exposure, pilot feasibility and network value carrying the highest weights.

## 5. The first commercial wedge

Do **not** lead with all ten FORTRESS engines.

Lead with one measurable problem:

> **“Can FORTRESS identify and explain risky transactions, beneficiaries, devices and behavioural patterns that are difficult to detect when each signal is analysed separately?”**

The standard pilot should combine:

- FORTRESS Transaction Risk Engine
- FORTRESS Scam Shield
- FORTRESS Device Engine
- FORTRESS Behaviour Engine
- FORTRESS Network Intelligence
- FORTRESS Case Engine
- FORTRESS Command Centre

The pilot remains sandbox-first and uses synthetic or lawfully provided controlled data. No autonomous movement, custody, freezing, recovery or settlement of customer funds is permitted.

## 6. First-contact sequence

### Contact 1 — Executive problem statement

Request a 30-minute discovery session with the executive responsible for fraud, financial crime, digital risk, payments, information security or technology.

Message architecture:

1. State the South African fraud problem.
2. State that FORTRESS is a decision/intelligence layer rather than a replacement core banking system.
3. Identify one concrete pilot use case.
4. Offer a controlled 90-day pilot.
5. Ask for the institution's highest-value fraud problem rather than assuming it.

### Contact 2 — Evidence pack

Send:

- Executive proposition
- Engine architecture
- Adversarial scenarios
- Acceptance methodology
- Decision API
- Threat/network intelligence design
- Case and investigation workflow
- Security boundary
- Data/privacy principles
- Pilot scope and success metrics

### Contact 3 — Technical validation

Run a technical workshop covering:

- available data signals
- API/event integration
- latency requirements
- decision outputs
- explainability
- privacy and retention
- security architecture
- model/rule governance
- audit requirements
- sandbox test design

### Contact 4 — Commercial/procurement

Offer the standard pilot:

- Foundation Pilot: R750,000
- Institutional Pilot: R1,000,000
- Complex/Multi-domain Pilot: R1,500,000+

Final pricing is subject to scope, data, integrations, SLA and procurement requirements.

## 7. Pilot success metrics

Every pilot must establish a baseline before intervention and measure:

- detection lift
- fraud-loss avoidance or exposure reduction
- false-positive rate
- decision latency
- intervention rate
- mule/network discovery
- scam detection rate
- investigation time
- analyst productivity
- explainability/audit completeness
- operational resilience

## 8. Procurement path

DISCOVER → ASSESS → SANDBOX → INTEGRATE → PROVE → EXECUTIVE REVIEW → PROCUREMENT → PRODUCTION → EXPAND

No production claim should be made until the institution has completed its own security, legal, regulatory, procurement and operational approvals.

## 9. Commercial objective

The first target is not the largest possible contract. The first target is a credible institutional reference and measurable proof of value.

Once one institution demonstrates measurable value, FORTRESS should use the evidence to approach the next institution with a stronger proposition:

**PROOF → REFERENCE → TRUST → SECOND PILOT → NETWORK EXPANSION → RECURRING REVENUE**

## 10. Non-negotiable positioning

FORTRESS is:

- a financial-crime defence and transaction-intelligence platform;
- an institutional decision and intelligence layer;
- explainable and auditable;
- privacy- and governance-conscious;
- designed for integration with authorised financial institutions and payment providers.

FORTRESS is not, by itself:

- a bank;
- a deposit taker;
- a payment institution;
- a settlement system;
- a custodian of customer funds;
- an autonomous financial controller.

Those regulated capabilities remain subject to the applicable licences, authorisations, partnerships and institutional controls.
