# FORTRESS™ — 90-DAY INSTITUTIONAL PILOT SOW

## 1. Purpose
This Statement of Work defines a controlled 90-day pilot of FORTRESS, IZAKHONO's financial-crime defence and transaction-intelligence platform.

The pilot is designed to determine whether FORTRESS can provide measurable incremental value against one agreed institutional financial-crime problem without disrupting production financial operations.

## 2. Pilot principle
**Measure first. Expand only when evidence supports expansion.**

The pilot is sandbox-first. No live-money movement, custody, settlement, autonomous fund recovery or autonomous account freezing is performed by FORTRESS.

## 3. Candidate use cases
The institution selects one primary use case, for example:

- Authorised-payment scam detection
- Account takeover
- Remote-access/RAT-enabled fraud
- New-beneficiary risk
- Mule-account/network discovery
- Coordinated fraud-ring detection
- Merchant compromise
- High-risk transaction escalation

## 4. Scope
### IZAKHONO / FORTRESS provides
- Risk-decision architecture
- Configurable risk rules and reason codes
- Identity/device/behaviour/transaction/network signal orchestration
- Controlled scenario replay
- Case-management workflow
- Threat/network intelligence analysis
- Audit evidence
- Pilot reporting
- Executive outcome review

### Institution provides
- Named executive sponsor
- Technical and fraud-risk stakeholders
- Approved test data or synthetic data
- Agreed integration/interface specifications
- Relevant baseline metrics where lawfully available
- Pilot acceptance criteria
- Timely review and feedback

## 5. Four phases
### Phase 1 — DISCOVER | Weeks 1–2
- Confirm problem statement
- Establish baseline
- Confirm data/privacy boundaries
- Define pilot architecture
- Agree success metrics
- Approve test scenarios

### Phase 2 — INTEGRATE | Weeks 3–6
- Connect approved signals
- Configure policies
- Map institutional decision outcomes
- Establish case workflow
- Validate audit trail
- Conduct security and access review

### Phase 3 — PROVE | Weeks 7–10
- Execute synthetic/adversarial scenarios
- Replay approved historical patterns where permitted
- Compare FORTRESS outputs with agreed baseline/control
- Measure detection, precision, latency and investigation productivity
- Document false positives and analyst overrides

### Phase 4 — EXECUTIVE REVIEW | Weeks 11–12
- Present measured results
- Review security/privacy evidence
- Review operational findings
- Identify production gaps
- Recommend: stop, extend, remediate or proceed toward production

## 6. Success metrics
The pilot should establish measurable results across:

1. Detection lift
2. False-positive control
3. Decision latency
4. Scam-intervention opportunities
5. Mule/network discovery
6. Investigation time
7. Analyst productivity
8. Reason-code completeness
9. Auditability
10. Integration effort

No percentage improvement is promised in advance. Results are measured against the agreed baseline.

## 8. Security and regulatory boundary
FORTRESS does not represent itself as a bank, payment institution, regulator or custodian of customer funds.

FORTRESS may generate risk recommendations and communicate permitted actions to authorised institutional systems. It does not independently move, custody, freeze, recover or settle funds.

Access is least-privilege. Privileged services remain server-side. Audit evidence is retained according to agreed governance requirements. Personal information is minimised and processed only within an agreed lawful basis and data-governance framework.

## 9. Pilot acceptance
The pilot is accepted when:

- agreed use case is demonstrated;
- agreed scenarios are replayable;
- decisions contain explainable reason codes;
- consequential decisions produce audit evidence;
- security controls are documented;
- no unauthorised live-money movement occurs;
- agreed metrics are reported;
- executive review is completed.

## 10. Outcome options
At the executive review, the parties may agree to:

**STOP** — evidence does not justify continuation.

**EXTEND** — additional evidence is required.

**REMEDIATE** — identified technical/operational gaps must be addressed.

**PROCEED** — evidence supports a separately approved production programme.

## 11. Core proposition
FORTRESS does not ask an institution to replace its existing fraud stack.

It asks a more practical question:

**Can an additional intelligence and decision layer connect signals that are currently fragmented and help investigators and authorised controls respond earlier, with greater context and better evidence?**

The pilot exists to answer that question with evidence.
