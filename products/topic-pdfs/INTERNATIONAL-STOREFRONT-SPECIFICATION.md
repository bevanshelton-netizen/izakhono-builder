# International Topic PDF Publishing Storefront Specification

**Programme:** $7 Topic PDF Publishing Programme  
**Owner and author:** Bevan Shelton  
**Status:** Product and experience specification — not a launch approval  
**Commercial boundary:** Standard target price US$7 per approved standalone guide  
**Programme boundary:** Separate from the full-length Book Publishing Programme and Global Campus

## 1. Purpose

Create an original, trustworthy, mobile-first international storefront for focused, original, practical knowledge guides. The storefront should help a reader identify a problem, understand what a guide contains, assess its relevance, purchase securely and receive the correct digital edition.

The business sells finished knowledge products to readers. It is not an author-services agency, a traditional vanity-publishing service, a university, a regulator or a professional advisory firm.

The Global Publishing Network website supplied as a reference is a commercial presentation benchmark only. We may learn from its clear positioning, service/product catalogue, how-it-works explanation, credibility content, FAQs, contact routes and social distribution. Do not copy its wording, logo, visual identity, layout, testimonials, assets or service model.

## 2. Positioning and brand principles

### Working value proposition

**Knowledge that moves you forward.**

Practical, carefully researched guides that help people understand everyday challenges and take informed next steps.

Supporting message: **Useful knowledge. Clear guidance. Practical next steps.**

These are working copy proposals. Final brand name, logo, domain, visual identity and any trademark clearance remain separate decisions; do not invent or register a brand without approval.

### Brand promises

- Original writing, not copied or repackaged material.
- A clearly defined reader problem and useful outcome for every guide.
- Transparent scope, country/region and language.
- Sources and review/update information where appropriate.
- Readable, professionally produced digital publications.
- Clear pricing, secure payment and dependable delivery.
- Honest claims: no guaranteed outcomes, invented credentials, fake reviews or unsupported endorsements.

## 3. Audience and catalogue structure

The storefront should be understandable to international readers and usable on mobile devices.

Initial catalogue categories may include:
- Personal finance and household management
- Business and entrepreneurship
- Careers and professional skills
- Education and learning
- Technology and digital skills
- Family and everyday living
- Personal development
- Practical home, food and lifestyle knowledge

Categories are navigation aids, not claims that every guide applies in every country. Each product must display its geographic scope and language. Legal, financial, health, safety, tax, employment and regulated topics require appropriate localisation and specialist review.

### Catalogue capabilities

- Browse all approved guides.
- Filter by category, language, country/region and reader need.
- Search by topic, problem or keyword.
- Show featured and recently updated guides without implying popularity unless supported by data.
- Display related guides and optional curated collections.
- Clearly distinguish available products from forthcoming or withdrawn titles.
- Never expose internal drafts, reviewer notes, source permissions or unpublished files.

## 4. Required site information architecture

1. **Home** — concise value proposition, categories, selected approved guides, quality promise and clear calls to action.
2. **Browse Guides** — searchable, filterable catalogue.
3. **Category pages** — explain the subject area and surface relevant guides.
4. **Product detail pages** — full product information, preview, price, edition and purchase action.
5. **Free Resources** — genuinely useful articles, sample pages or downloadable resources.
6. **Our Standards** — research, editorial review, rights, corrections and update policy.
7. **About** — truthful publisher identity, purpose and contact information.
8. **Help / FAQ** — purchasing, file formats, delivery, access, refunds, accessibility and support.
9. **Contact** — support channel and expected response window once operationally defined.
10. **Customer access** — secure purchase lookup or account/library, subject to privacy-conscious design.
11. **Legal and policy pages** — privacy, terms of sale, refund/cancellation, digital delivery, accessibility, cookies and other jurisdictionally required notices.

Do not publish placeholder contact details, fabricated office addresses, invented staff profiles or policy claims that have not been reviewed.

## 5. Homepage content sequence

### Hero

**Knowledge that moves you forward.**

Practical, carefully researched guides to help you understand challenges, make informed decisions and take meaningful action.

Primary action: **Explore the Guides**  
Secondary action: **Read a Free Resource**

A visible price cue may say **Focused guides from US$7**, once the commercial configuration is approved and the price is accurate.

### Homepage sections

1. Hero and primary actions.
2. Browse by topic.
3. Featured approved guides with cover, short promise, scope and price.
4. How it works: Discover → Preview → Purchase → Receive → Put knowledge into practice.
5. Why readers can trust the publishing process.
6. Free educational resources.
7. Related guide collections, if available.
8. Verified reader experiences, only when genuine permissioned feedback exists.
9. Frequently asked questions.
10. Contact and complete legal/footer information.

Do not show empty testimonial carousels, fake counters, fabricated review stars, false scarcity, invented bestseller labels or countdown timers.

## 6. Product detail page requirements

Every product page must display:

- Product title and subtitle.
- Professional cover image with appropriate usage rights.
- Author and actual contributors/roles.
- Concise description of the reader's problem.
- Who the guide is for and who it is not for.
- What the reader will learn or be able to do.
- Contents or a meaningful chapter/section outline.
- Practical tools included (worksheets, checklists, examples) only if they are actually in the file.
- Country/region, language and edition.
- File format, approximate page count and accessibility information when verified.
- Sample preview with deliberate limits and no accidental disclosure of the entire paid file.
- Sources/research approach or a link to the publication's references where appropriate.
- Publication/version date and last reviewed date.
- Scope limitations and relevant professional-advice boundary.
- Actual price, currency, taxes/fees disclosure as required.
- Secure purchase action and clear post-purchase delivery explanation.
- Support and refund-policy links.

Product pages must not promise results the guide cannot guarantee. Claims must be supported by the publication and its review record.

## 7. Purchase, payment and delivery requirements

- Use a reputable payment provider selected after commercial, regulatory, availability and fee assessment.
- Support international customers and clearly disclose the currency charged.
- Show taxes, conversion and other charges as required before payment.
- Provide an order confirmation and receipt.
- Deliver the exact purchased product edition through a secure, reliable method.
- Prevent public indexing of paid download URLs where technically feasible.
- Make re-download and lost-access support clear.
- Record product version and purchase entitlement so material corrections can be communicated.
- Handle refunds, chargebacks, failed payments and customer support transparently.
- Minimise personal data collection and retain it only as needed under an approved retention policy.
- Do not sell or share customer data for unrelated marketing.
- Obtain consent for marketing communications; purchases must not silently subscribe a customer.
- Test failed, duplicate, refunded and interrupted transactions before launch.

No payment account, paid subscription, domain, hosting, advertising, vendor contract or infrastructure spend may be initiated without the owner's explicit approval. This specification does not authorise live payments or deployment.

## 8. Reader trust and publishing standards

Create a public **Our Publishing Standards** page explaining, in plain language:

- How topics are selected.
- How research and sources are assessed.
- How original writing is produced.
- How factual and editorial reviews are performed.
- When specialist review is required.
- How third-party quotations, images, fonts and other assets are licensed or attributed.
- How real people's stories and personal data are protected.
- How corrections, updates and withdrawals are handled.
- How readers can report an error.

The public explanation must match actual operational practice. Do not claim independent review, professional certification, institutional approval or specialist endorsement unless documented.

## 9. Social proof and free resources

### Reader feedback

- Collect feedback only from genuine readers.
- Verify that the reviewer actually purchased or used the publication before applying a verified-reader label.
- Obtain permission for names, photos, video and quotations.
- Disclose incentives or material relationships.
- Preserve the original feedback record.
- Do not edit feedback in a way that changes its meaning.
- Do not fabricate testimonials or imply that one person's experience is typical.

### Free resources

Free resources should demonstrate quality and provide real value. Each should have a defined purpose and editorial review. A newsletter subscription must be optional, transparent and consent-based.

## 10. Internationalisation and accessibility

- Design for responsive mobile, tablet and desktop use.
- Use plain, readable language and accessible navigation.
- Provide meaningful image alt text, keyboard access, sufficient contrast and semantic structure.
- Test screen-reader and zoom behaviour.
- Identify product language and edition prominently.
- Introduce translations in phases using qualified translators and independent language review.
- Localise examples, legal references, units, currency and institutional details rather than mechanically translating.
- Do not claim coverage of every language until each edition actually exists and has passed review.
- Make clear when a guide is country-specific and when it is general educational information.

## 11. Analytics and operational reporting

Track only what is useful and lawful, with privacy-respecting analytics:

- Catalogue visits and product-page visits.
- Search terms and zero-result searches.
- Preview engagement.
- Add-to-checkout and completed-purchase conversion.
- Sales by product, country/region and language where lawfully available.
- Refunds, chargebacks and support issues.
- Download/access failures.
- Reader feedback and correction reports.
- Repeat purchase and related-guide discovery.

Do not treat impressions, followers or downloads as revenue. Report gross sales separately from net proceeds after taxes, refunds, payment fees, currency conversion and operating costs.

## 12. Launch gates

The storefront is not ready for public commerce until:

- Publisher identity and public brand copy are approved.
- A small initial collection of guides has passed all editorial, rights, specialist and PDF QA gates.
- Product metadata and product pages match the actual files.
- Legal policies have been reviewed for the operating entity and intended markets.
- Payment provider and payout arrangements have been approved and tested.
- Digital delivery, refunds, support and security have been tested end to end.
- Accessibility and mobile QA have been completed.
- Analytics and privacy notices are accurate.
- No placeholder, false claim, draft or unpublished product is visible.
- The owner explicitly approves launch and any associated expenditure.

## 13. Technical implementation boundaries

Implement the storefront as a distinct module within the existing IZAKHONO Builder repository, reusing approved shared infrastructure only where appropriate. Keep its catalogue and publishing workflows separate from the full-length Book Publishing Programme and Global Campus.

Prefer a content-driven catalogue backed by the product records. Do not hardcode product prices or publication status in multiple places. Product availability must derive from the approved publication state and release gates.

Initial work may include documentation, local development, mock product pages, tests and non-production previews. It must not activate payments, collect live customer data, deploy publicly or incur costs without explicit approval.

## 14. Acceptance criteria

- A reader can understand the publisher's proposition within seconds.
- A reader can browse and find a guide by topic.
- Every product page answers what it is, who it is for, what it contains, where it applies, what it costs and how delivery works.
- The storefront clearly distinguishes country and language editions.
- Only approved products are purchasable.
- The user journey works on mobile and desktop.
- No fake testimonials, unsupported claims, copied GPN assets or placeholder policies appear.
- Purchase and delivery tests pass before any live transaction capability is enabled.
- The product catalogue remains separate from full-length books and Global Campus.
