## Before you begin

Explain any information, materials, tools or decisions the reader needs before using the guide.

## 1. Understand the problem

Describe the problem in practical, recognisable terms. Explain key concepts and common misconceptions without shaming the reader.

## 2. The method

Present the main framework or process in a logical sequence. Explain why each step matters and what the reader should do.

## 3. Work through an example

Use an original, clearly identified fictional or composite example where useful. Do not present invented people, results or quotations as real testimonials or research.

## 4. Put it into practice

Give the reader a manageable action sequence, checklist, worksheet, decision aid or reusable framework. Ensure every promised tool is actually included.

## 5. Common obstacles and adjustments

Address likely difficulties, alternatives and realistic ways to adapt the method. Distinguish general guidance from jurisdiction-specific requirements.

## 6. Your action plan

Provide a short plan the reader can begin, with sensible steps and review points. Keep it proportionate to the topic.

## Closing

Summarise the central practical lesson and give the reader a clear next step. Do not repeat the entire guide or add unsupported sales claims.
