# International Go-to-Market Playbook

**Programme:** $7 Topic PDF Publishing Programme  
**Owner:** Bevan Shelton  
**Status:** Planning and execution guide — not a launch or advertising-spend authorisation  
**Reference:** Global Publishing Network website supplied by the owner, used only as a high-level commercial presentation benchmark

## 1. Market objective

Build a sustainable international direct-to-reader publishing business around original, focused, useful digital guides. The central commercial proposition is a practical guide at a target standard price of US$7.

The customer buys a useful answer, framework or practical method—not a file extension or a promise of guaranteed results.

## 2. Market positioning

Working message:

**Knowledge that moves you forward.**

Supporting proposition:

**Practical, carefully researched guides that help you understand challenges, make informed decisions and take meaningful action.**

The positioning must be:
- Reader-problem-led rather than publisher-centred.
- Clear about the specific benefit and scope of each publication.
- Evidence-aware and honest about limitations.
- International in ambition, with country-specific editions where required.
- Distinct from the full-length book catalogue and from author-publishing services.

Final brand name, identity, domain and trademark review remain to be decided and approved.

## 3. Market segments

Prioritise audiences by the actual topic and evidence of demand, not by assuming one guide serves everyone.

Potential segments include:
- Households seeking practical everyday financial organisation.
- New and existing small-business owners.
- Students and lifelong learners.
- Workers developing career and digital skills.
- Parents and caregivers seeking practical everyday information.
- Individuals seeking structured approaches to common life and work challenges.

For each guide, record:
- Primary reader segment.
- Reader's urgent question or problem.
- Search language and common phrasing.
- Geographic scope.
- Existing alternatives and differentiation.
- Evidence of demand.
- What the guide can and cannot help the reader achieve.

## 4. Product-led marketing

Each guide needs its own campaign based on its specific reader problem.

A campaign pack should include:
1. One-sentence reader promise.
2. Short and long product descriptions.
3. Product cover and a consistent set of promotional graphics.
4. A preview/sample with permission-cleared material.
5. Three to five educational social posts.
6. One short-video script or narrated explainer.
7. One search-oriented article or resource page.
8. One email announcement, where consent-based email marketing is in place.
9. Related-guide recommendations only when genuinely relevant.
10. A list of claims and sources for factual marketing statements.

The marketing must accurately represent the guide. Never advertise a worksheet, expert review, template, country coverage or result that is not actually present.

## 5. Acquisition channels

### Organic search

- Create useful, original articles answering real reader questions.
- Use descriptive titles, headings and product metadata.
- Link educational resources to relevant guides without disguising advertising.
- Build topic/category pages with genuine explanatory value.
- Keep country-specific search pages distinct where the material differs.
- Review search queries and update content as reader needs change.

### Social platforms

Use suitable channels such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Pinterest and YouTube according to the target audience and subject.

Publish helpful material rather than constant purchase requests:
- Explain one useful concept.
- Demonstrate a practical step.
- Share a clearly labelled fictional/composite example when appropriate.
- Answer common questions.
- Show a legitimate preview of the guide.
- Invite readers to explore the full publication.

Maintain a content calendar and measure clicks and sales rather than relying on follower counts.

### Email

- Offer a useful free resource.
- Obtain clear opt-in consent.
- State what subscribers will receive and how often.
- Include a simple unsubscribe mechanism.
- Send relevant educational content and occasional product announcements.
- Do not add purchasers to promotional lists without an appropriate lawful basis and consent where required.

### Partnerships and referrals

Potential partners include educators, professional communities, topic-focused creators, community organisations and relevant websites.

Use written terms for paid referrals, commissions, affiliate disclosure, use of brand assets and customer data. Do not imply endorsement by a partner without permission.

### Marketplaces and third-party distributors

Evaluate marketplaces as supplementary distribution, not as a substitute for owning the core brand, catalogue and direct customer relationship. Compare fees, payout access, territory availability, tax handling, customer-data access, refund rules and platform restrictions before listing.

### Paid advertising

Paid campaigns are a later-stage experiment, not the first launch dependency. Begin only after:
- The product has passed quality and rights gates.
- The landing page and checkout are tested.
- Organic traffic or small controlled tests indicate a credible offer.
- Tracking and refund reporting are working.
- A maximum budget and stop-loss rule are approved.

No advertising or other expenditure is authorised by this playbook.

## 6. Product page conversion framework

Every product page should answer:

1. **Is this for me?** — identify the reader and their situation.
2. **What problem does it address?** — explain the problem in recognisable language.
3. **What will I get?** — describe the actual contents, examples and tools.
4. **Why should I trust it?** — explain sources, editorial standards, scope and review.
5. **Does it apply where I live?** — show country/region and edition.
6. **What does it cost?** — display the actual price and currency clearly.
7. **What happens after payment?** — explain format and delivery.
8. **What if I need help?** — link to support and refund information.

Use a preview to reduce purchase uncertainty. The preview should be representative and must not expose the complete paid publication.

## 7. Trust and credibility

Use the commercial presentation strengths of the supplied GPN reference as inspiration: clear homepage, organised catalogue, simple process explanation, why-choose-us section, FAQs, contact information, policies and social channels.

Improve the trust model through visible editorial transparency:
- Public publishing standards.
- Clear author and reviewer attribution.
- Verifiable sources and update dates.
- Rights and permissions discipline.
- A visible correction/report-an-error route.
- Genuine reader feedback with consent.
- Honest explanation of what the guide does not replace.

Never manufacture reviews, video testimonials, sales counts, awards, partner logos, professional credentials, press mentions or popularity claims.

## 8. Launch catalogue strategy

Do not launch with a large number of thin or unreviewed documents. Build a small, coherent collection of fully completed products. A practical planning target is three to five launch-ready guides, but only if each independently passes the complete release checklist. Quality gates take precedence over a numerical target.

Choose the initial collection so that:
- Each product solves a distinct problem.
- The group makes sense under clear catalogue categories.
- Each product has a defined reader and scope.
- The initial set demonstrates editorial consistency.
- Country-specific guides are labelled honestly.
- There is no duplication with the separate full-length book catalogue.

Do not make a candidate topic public merely because it appears in the internal queue.

## 9. Launch sequence

### Phase A — Foundation
- Approve brand identity and public positioning.
- Complete storefront information architecture and product-page template.
- Publish the editorial standards and support/policy content after review.
- Complete the initial guide collection and campaign assets.
- Confirm payment, delivery, tax and customer-support arrangements.

### Phase B — Closed quality testing
- Test with a small invited group without representing the store as publicly launched.
- Ask users to test navigation, preview clarity, mobile usability and product comprehension.
- If test purchases are used, use a properly configured test environment or an approved controlled process.
- Record issues and resolve critical problems before public sale.

### Phase C — Public launch
- Publish only approved guides.
- Announce the launch through owned channels and relevant partner channels.
- Publish educational content for each launch guide.
- Monitor customer support and delivery closely.
- Avoid unsupported claims about sales, popularity or customer outcomes.

### Phase D — Optimisation
- Review search terms, page engagement and conversion.
- Improve unclear product descriptions and previews.
- Identify missing topics from genuine reader questions.
- Update guides when sources, facts, regulations or reader needs change.
- Expand languages and regions through reviewed localisation.

## 10. Measurement

Report these measures separately:

**Discovery**
- Search impressions and clicks.
- Visits by source/channel.
- Catalogue searches and zero-result searches.

**Consideration**
- Product-page engagement.
- Preview views.
- Related-guide clicks.
- Add-to-cart or checkout starts, if supported.

**Commerce**
- Completed orders.
- Units sold by guide and edition.
- Gross revenue.
- Refunds and chargebacks.
- Payment fees, taxes and currency-conversion costs.
- Net proceeds.

**Customer experience**
- Delivery success and failed downloads.
- Support requests and response times.
- Reader feedback and complaints.
- Repeat purchases and relevant cross-discovery.

Do not confuse gross revenue with profit. Do not optimise solely for conversion if that would make claims misleading or harm reader trust.

## 11. Pricing and commercial rules

The standard target price is US$7 per individual guide. Any local currency display, tax treatment, discounts, bundles, subscriptions, institutional licences or wholesale terms require separate commercial design and approval.

Do not promise a fixed exchange-rate equivalent. Display the actual charge currency and any applicable taxes/fees clearly at checkout.

## 12. Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Thin or inaccurate content | Editorial and factual review before release |
| Country-specific advice sold globally | Prominent jurisdiction labels and localisation |
| Copyright infringement | Original writing, source ledger and asset permissions |
| False social proof | Verified feedback and written permission |
| Low conversion | Better previews, clearer promises and measured tests |
| Payment or delivery failure | End-to-end test cases and support process |
| Privacy breach | Data minimisation, access controls and reviewed policies |
| Translation errors | Qualified translation and independent review |
| Ad spend without evidence | Approved budget, stop-loss and owner authorisation |
| Catalogue fragmentation | Shared metadata, taxonomy and product governance |

## 13. Owner approvals required before launch

The following remain explicit approval points:
- Final publishing brand name and identity.
- Domain and any registration costs.
- Hosting, infrastructure or paid subscriptions.
- Payment provider, account setup and fees.
- Legal entity, tax and cross-border compliance arrangements.
- Public launch date.
- Advertising budget and paid campaigns.
- Marketplace distribution agreements.
- Any paid translation, editorial or specialist-review engagement.
