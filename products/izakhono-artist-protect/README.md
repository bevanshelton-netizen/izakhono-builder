# IZAKHONO ARTIST PROTECT

Creator-rights, contract-readiness, royalty-registration and financial-survival rails for IZAKHONO music/media products.

## Rights are separated

ARTIST PROTECT never treats these as one right:

1. composition / musical work;
2. master / sound recording;
3. performer / needletime interests;
4. mechanical/reproduction rights;
5. the limited licence granted to an IZAKHONO platform.

## Clearance gate

A release cannot become `cleared` until identity, master ownership, 100% composition splits, 100% master splits, required contributor signatures, sample/beat/cover permissions, minor consent where relevant, platform licence and dispute checks are complete.

Radio clearance is a separate gate.

## Contract Shield

The system supplies assistance templates but does not pretend a generic template replaces an attorney.

Automatic lawyer escalation applies to high-risk matters including:
- copyright or master assignment;
- broad/perpetual exclusivity;
- 360 participation;
- broad powers of attorney;
- uncapped indemnities;
- cross-collateralisation;
- unclear royalty bases;
- missing audit/accounting rights;
- management deals and major long-term options.

## Registration guide

Depending on role, the artist is prompted to investigate:
- SAMRO for performing-right administration of musical works;
- CAPASSO for mechanical-right administration of musical works;
- SAMPRA for needletime/neighbouring rights of recording artists/record companies.

## Dispute Shield

A dispute freezes the disputed right/share, preserves evidence and creates a takedown/escalation trail. Undisputed shares are not automatically forfeited.

## Artist Survival

Each payout may be displayed as:
- estimated tax reserve;
- emergency reserve;
- reinvestment;
- available-to-spend.

These are budgeting reserves, not tax assessments. Complex tax matters are escalated to a registered tax practitioner.

## Legal baseline

South African copyright assignments and exclusive licences require writing and signature. Electronic contracting/signatures must meet applicable ECTA requirements. Performer consents, ownership records and platform licences are stored with a tamper-evident audit reference.

Final legal wording for high-value or unusual deals should be reviewed by a South African entertainment/IP attorney.
