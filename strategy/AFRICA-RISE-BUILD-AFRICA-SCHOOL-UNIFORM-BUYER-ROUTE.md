# BUILD AFRICA — School and Corporate Uniform Buyer Route

**Status:** Internal pilot planning document. This is a buyer-discovery route, not evidence of demand, a supplier appointment, a tender award, or an approved sales campaign.

## Objective

Validate whether a small, controlled manufacturing pilot can match verified South African buyers with capable manufacturers on transparent specifications, fair pricing, acceptable quality, realistic lead times and workable payment terms.

The first product family for validation is standard school and corporate uniforms. No capacity, stock, price, delivery date, buyer, contract or revenue is assumed.

## Buyer channels to validate

1. **Independent uniform retailers and outfitters** — establish whether they need additional local manufacturing capacity, repeatable basic garments, private-label production or overflow support.
2. **Private employers and industrial businesses** — identify uniform requirements, wear-and-tear expectations, size ranges, replenishment patterns and delivery locations.
3. **Schools and school governing bodies** — engage only through transparent, competitive and lawful procurement processes. Do not offer or seek arrangements that improperly restrict parents' choice.
4. **Public-sector opportunities** — monitor official tender portals and pursue only opportunities for which the legal entity, registrations, local-content evidence, manufacturing capacity, experience, pricing and submission requirements are verified. A tender notice is not a sales lead until eligibility and deadlines are checked against the official bid pack.
5. **Clubs, academies and community organisations** — validate smaller, specification-led orders that can be produced without speculative inventory.

## School-uniform competition and consumer safeguards

South Africa's Competition Commission published FAQ material on 9 January 2026 concerning its school-uniform and learning-material procurement guidelines. The National Consumer Commission also summarises the principles that schools should use competitive procurement, limit exclusivity to genuinely necessary items (for example, school badges), avoid excessively long supplier agreements and preserve consumer choice.

Accordingly, the pilot must:
- compete on quality, price, service and delivery rather than exclusivity;
- distinguish generic uniform items from genuinely school-specific branded items;
- never represent that parents must buy generic items from one nominated outlet;
- avoid collusive conduct, bid coordination, market allocation or price discussions with competing suppliers;
- keep every quotation specific, itemised, time-limited and based on the same written specification;
- obtain legal/procurement review before any school or public-sector bid or supply agreement.

Reference material:
- Competition Commission FAQ, 9 January 2026: https://www.compcom.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/School-Uniform-Procurement-Guidelines-FAQ-.pdf
- National Consumer Commission consumer alert: https://thencc.org.za/consumer-alert-procurement-of-school-uniforms-and-other-learning-related-goods-and-services/

## Buyer discovery sequence

### Step 1 — Establish the buyer's actual need
Ask the buyer to provide:
- garment/item description and intended use;
- size breakdown and size chart;
- fabric composition, weight, colour and performance requirements;
- branding, badge, embroidery and labelling requirements;
- sample or approved reference garment;
- estimated quantity and whether it is a one-off or repeat requirement;
- delivery location, required date and delivery split;
- quality acceptance criteria, returns/defect process and packaging;
- procurement route, decision-maker and authorised signatory;
- payment terms and any supplier onboarding requirements.

Do not treat an indicative quantity or verbal interest as an order.

### Step 2 — Verify the manufacturing match
For each prospective manufacturer, record:
- legal entity and authorised representative;
- actual production site and equipment;
- current available capacity and production schedule;
- workforce and relevant skills;
- materials sourcing, minimum order quantities and lead times;
- sample-making and quality-control process;
- subcontracting arrangements, if any;
- applicable registrations, insurance, certifications and their scope/expiry;
- delivery, packaging and after-sales capability;
- references and evidence of comparable completed work.

No accreditation or certification may be claimed without a current document and scope check.

### Step 3 — Cost the exact specification
Prepare an itemised cost sheet for each SKU:
- fabric and trims;
- cutting, sewing and finishing labour;
- printing, embroidery, labels and packaging;
- quality checks, rework allowance and wastage;
- transport and delivery;
- applicable tax treatment;
- working-capital requirement and payment timing;
- contingency and approved margin.

A quote is not released until the specification, cost assumptions, capacity, delivery plan and approval are recorded.

### Step 4 — Sample and approve
Produce a sample only against an agreed specification and authorised sample budget. Record fit, measurements, fabric, colour, construction, branding, wash/performance tests where relevant, defects and written buyer approval.

### Step 5 — Order acceptance gate
Accept an order only when all are true:
- buyer identity and authority verified;
- written purchase order or signed agreement received;
- final specification and sample approval recorded;
- capacity and materials confirmed;
- price and margin approved;
- payment and working-capital exposure acceptable;
- delivery schedule achievable;
- quality and remedy terms understood;
- any tender, school procurement or regulatory requirements checked.

## Pilot scorecard

Track actual evidence, not optimistic estimates:
- qualified buyer conversations completed;
- written specifications received;
- manufacturer capability files verified;
- samples requested, produced and approved;
- comparable quotations issued;
- orders formally received;
- on-time-in-full delivery;
- first-pass quality acceptance and defect/rework rate;
- gross contribution after direct costs;
- payment days and cash conversion;
- repeat-order intent and realised repeat orders;
- complaints, returns and corrective actions.

## Stop conditions

Pause the relevant opportunity if:
- buyer requirements remain vague or change without written control;
- production capacity or quality cannot be evidenced;
- the required standard or certification is missing or outside scope;
- pricing depends on unverified fabric, labour or transport assumptions;
- payment terms create unacceptable cash exposure;
- a buyer requests improper exclusivity, collusion, bid manipulation or misleading claims;
- the order would compromise another confirmed commitment.

## Governance and related-party safeguards

AFRICA RISE must disclose any relationship between the programme administrator and a manufacturer being considered. If an IZAKHONO-related business is evaluated, apply the same verification, quotation and quality requirements as to other manufacturers; keep programme administration and manufacturing accounts separate; document the relationship; and use independent review for related-party selection and pricing. AFRICA RISE must not be presented as an independent marketplace unless its governance and operating arrangements genuinely support that claim.

## First operating cycle

1. Complete the manufacturer capability register with evidence.
2. Identify a balanced discovery sample across retailers, employers, schools and organisations; do not imply endorsement or existing relationships.
3. Conduct structured buyer interviews and obtain written specifications where possible.
4. Match only verified specifications to verified manufacturing capacity.
5. Prepare sample and quotation economics.
6. Review results and decide whether a tightly capped pilot is commercially and operationally justified.

**Decision rule:** If buyer demand, compliant procurement route, manufacturing capability, acceptable unit economics and delivery readiness cannot all be evidenced, do not launch the pilot or commit funds.
