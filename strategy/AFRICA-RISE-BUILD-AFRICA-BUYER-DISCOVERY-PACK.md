# AFRICA RISE — BUILD AFRICA Buyer Discovery Pack

**Status:** Internal, non-binding preparation material  
**Purpose:** Validate whether real school and corporate buyers have a defined uniform requirement before any quotation, sample production or pilot approval.  
**Use:** Adapt and send only through an authorised IZAKHONO/AFRICA RISE representative. This pack does not authorise external outreach, represent a legal entity, create an agency relationship or promise supply.

## 1. Non-negotiable positioning

AFRICA RISE is exploring a controlled buyer-to-manufacturer matching pilot. It is not yet an approved procurement intermediary or public programme with an adopted legal structure. Do not claim that AFRICA RISE has approved manufacturers, reserved capacity, confirmed buyers, secured funding or guaranteed delivery.

Where IZAKHONO Africa is discussed as a possible manufacturer, disclose the relationship clearly. Do not imply that AFRICA RISE independently endorses or has competitively selected IZAKHONO. Any related-party evaluation requires an authorised independent review.

## 2. First-contact email

**Subject:** Non-binding market research — school and corporate uniform requirements

Good day,

We are conducting preliminary market research to understand current school and corporate uniform requirements and the practical standards suppliers must meet.

Would you be willing to take part in a short, non-binding discussion? We would like to understand:

- The garment categories and specifications you typically procure
- Approximate quantities and size ranges
- Your procurement calendar and delivery windows
- Fabric, branding, durability and sample-approval requirements
- Supplier onboarding, compliance and quality requirements
- How quotations are evaluated and what payment terms apply

This is discovery only. It is not a quotation, tender response, offer to supply or request for a purchase commitment. No supplier appointment or pilot has been approved.

If useful, we can arrange a brief discussion at a time convenient to you. Please also let us know if there is a formal procurement contact or public supplier-onboarding process we should follow.

Kind regards,  
[Authorised representative]  
[Approved organisation and contact details]

We are trying to understand the specifications, quantities, quality requirements, procurement calendar and supplier onboarding process buyers actually use. This is a non-binding research discussion, not a sales pitch or tender approach. Would you be the right person to speak to, or could you direct us to the appropriate procurement contact?

Do not send bulk messages, scrape contact details or follow up after an opt-out.

## 4. 20-minute buyer interview

### Opening and permission
1. Confirm the person's role and whether they are authorised to discuss procurement processes.
2. Explain the research purpose and that participation is voluntary.
3. Ask permission to take factual notes. Do not record calls without explicit consent.
4. Ask whether the organisation permits follow-up and how it prefers to be contacted.

### Requirement
5. Which garments are procured? (e.g. shirts, trousers, skirts, jerseys, blazers, tracksuits, jackets)
6. What are the written specifications: fabric composition/weight, colour, construction, trims, branding and labelling?
7. What size range and size mix are required?
8. What quantities are typical, and are they confirmed or only forecast?
9. Are there seasonal or annual procurement windows?

### Quality and delivery
10. What samples, wear tests, wash tests, inspections or acceptance criteria are mandatory?
11. What are the delivery location, delivery schedule and packaging requirements?
12. What happens when goods fail inspection or sizes/specifications are incorrect?

### Procurement and commercial controls
13. What supplier registration, tax, insurance, health-and-safety, B-BBEE or other documentation is required for this specific buyer and contract?
14. Is procurement by quotation, panel, tender, framework agreement or another route?
15. What are the evaluation criteria and decision process?
16. What are the payment terms, invoice requirements and any retention/penalty provisions?
17. Is there a formal, published route for supplier onboarding or future opportunities?

### Close
18. May we receive the current written specification or a public supplier pack?
19. May we contact you again about this specific research?
20. What would make a new supplier unsuitable, regardless of price?

## 5. Qualification standard

Do not label a conversation a qualified opportunity unless the evidence includes:

- Identifiable organisation and legitimate procurement route
- Actual requirement, not general interest
- Written specification or documented path to obtain it
- Quantity and size mix, or a credible method/date for confirmation
- Required delivery date and destination
- Sample and acceptance criteria
- Supplier onboarding and compliance requirements
- Quotation/evaluation process and payment terms
- Permission for the stated follow-up

A catalogue request, informal expression of interest, public tender notice or unverified forecast is not an order.

## 6. Evidence and data handling

- Record organisation-level information and business contact details only where necessary and lawfully obtained.
- Record the source of each contact and the permission/status for follow-up.
- Do not collect identity numbers, personal financial information, sensitive personal information or unnecessary personal data.
- Keep notes in an access-controlled approved system; do not place private contact details in public repository files.
- Do not publish buyer names, logos, specifications, volumes, budgets or procurement intentions without written permission.
- Retain source documents only where authorised; record a secure evidence reference rather than copying credentials into a general register.
- Follow the organisation's approved privacy notice, retention schedule and security controls. If these are not approved, keep this as an offline internal template and do not create a public intake channel.

## 8. Stop conditions

Stop and escalate rather than quote or promise supply if:
- The buyer's procurement route is unclear or appears to bypass applicable rules.
- The request involves an active tender and the correct communication channel is unknown.
- Required specifications, delivery dates or acceptance criteria are missing.
- Capacity, materials, working capital, quality or delivery cannot be evidenced.
- The request includes PPE or protective claims without exact standards and valid scope-specific certification.
- A conflict of interest is undisclosed or an independent review is unavailable.
- The buyer requests an unauthorised commission, inducement, facilitation payment or misleading representation.
- The buyer has not consented to further contact or asks not to be contacted.

## 9. Approval path before any commercial commitment

1. Confirm legal entity, authority and programme governance.
2. Complete conflict declarations and independent review where related parties are involved.
3. Verify the buyer's requirement and procurement route.
4. Verify manufacturer identity, capability, quality, statutory and certification evidence.
5. Cost the actual specification, including materials, labour, branding, packaging, logistics, tax, wastage, financing and risk.
6. Confirm cash flow and working capital against payment terms.
7. Produce and obtain written sample approval where required.
8. Score and submit the complete pilot dossier to the authorised decision-maker.
9. Sign only through the verified contracting entity and authorised signatory.
