# AFRICA RISE — BUILD AFRICA First Pilot Candidate

**Recommended first pilot to validate:** Verified Manufacturing Capability and Buyer-Match Pilot  
**Proposed anchor capability:** Izakhono Africa clothing and textile manufacturing  
**Decision status:** Candidate selected for validation, not yet approved for live execution.

## 1. Why this is the first candidate

BUILD AFRICA can start with a tangible product, a defined buyer, a production specification and a measurable commercial transaction. This is a shorter and more controllable first proof than launching a new regulated telecommunications service or a multi-country trade platform.

The proposed anchor capability is the existing IZAKHONO manufacturing operation. The known product categories include uniforms, matric jackets, PPE and sportswear. These categories provide a starting catalogue for buyer discovery; current equipment, capacity, certifications, materials, lead times and quality performance must be re-verified before any offer is made.

## 2. Pilot objective

Prove a repeatable path from:
**verified buyer requirement → qualified manufacturer → costed quotation → approved sample/specification → purchase order → quality control → delivery → payment reconciliation → buyer feedback.**

The pilot should establish whether AFRICA RISE can help a capable African manufacturer access qualified procurement opportunities and fulfil them reliably, while preserving the manufacturer's commercial independence and protecting buyer trust.

## 3. Initial scope

### Include
- One product family initially, selected from uniforms, PPE, sportswear or matric jackets.
- One defined buyer segment, such as schools, employers, institutions or corporate procurement.
- A small number of real buyer briefs.
- Verification of the manufacturing entity, ownership, relevant registrations and capability.
- Product specifications, material standards, size ranges, sample approval and quality-control plan.
- Costed quotation including materials, labour, overhead, packaging, delivery, tax and contingency.
- Purchase-order and payment terms reviewed before acceptance.
- Production and delivery evidence, customer acceptance and post-delivery review.

### Exclude until separately approved
- Claims of national or continental manufacturing capacity.
- Public-sector tender representations or use of government logos.
- Products requiring certifications or safety approvals not held and verified.
- Large orders beyond verified capacity or working capital.
- Financing, guarantees, credit extension or inventory purchases without approval.
- Any grant-funded or subsidised order without eligibility and restricted-fund review.

## 4. Independence and conflict controls

Because the proposed anchor manufacturer is part of the IZAKHONO ecosystem, the pilot must be transparent about related-party interests.

- Disclose the relationship to buyers and any funder where relevant.
- Keep AFRICA RISE programme administration and the manufacturer's sales/production accounts distinct.
- Do not use the programme to imply independent certification or preferential procurement status.
- Apply the same supplier verification, quality and pricing tests that would apply to another manufacturer.
- Record conflicts and require an independent reviewer for decisions involving programme funds, referrals or supplier selection.
- Never represent an introduction or listing as a guarantee of procurement or award.

AFRICA RISE should be capable of onboarding other qualified African manufacturers on the same published criteria. It must not become a disguised marketing channel for one related company.

## 5. Buyer discovery

Start with direct discovery, not mass advertising.

Target buyer groups:
- private schools and education groups;
- private employers and industrial businesses;
- security, facilities and service companies;
- sports clubs and academies;
- corporate procurement teams;
- authorised public procurement opportunities only after tender-specific compliance review.

Ask each prospective buyer:
1. What product is required and for which users?
2. What is the complete technical specification and standard?
3. What quantities and delivery dates are genuinely required?
4. What sample, inspection and acceptance process applies?
5. What is the procurement and approval process?
6. What payment terms and supplier onboarding documents are required?
7. What failed with previous suppliers?
8. What would make a supplier reliable enough for repeat orders?

Do not publish buyer names, logos, order values or procurement intentions without permission.

## 6. Manufacturer verification pack

Before quoting, assemble and verify:
- legal entity and authorised contracting details;
- tax and company compliance status where relevant to the buyer;
- product catalogue and current specifications;
- production equipment and process inventory;
- actual monthly capacity by product and shift;
- workforce and critical skills availability;
- current supplier/material sources and lead times;
- quality-control procedures and sample records;
- relevant accreditations, certificates and their current scope/validity;
- health, safety and environmental practices;
- insurance where required;
- references and completed-work evidence where permission exists;
- delivery, packaging and after-sales capability;
- working-capital requirements and production constraints.

A certification must be described exactly as issued; never imply that accreditation in one field certifies every product.

## 7. Unit economics and quotation discipline

Every quotation must show the calculation and approval:
- material consumption and current supplier quote;
- direct labour hours and rate;
- production overhead allocation;
- quality inspection and rework allowance;
- packaging and labelling;
- freight and delivery;
- tax treatment;
- financing/working-capital cost where material;
- contingency and target gross margin.

No standard selling price is to be invented for the pilot. Use current supplier quotations, approved wage assumptions, actual production time and current buyer specifications. Reconcile actual cost after delivery to improve the next quote.

## 8. 30-day execution sprint

### Days 1–5 — Verify capability
- Confirm the responsible legal entity and authorised signatory.
- Gather company, tax, accreditation and insurance evidence.
- Inventory equipment, processes, current capacity and constraints.
- Select one product family and produce a technical specification sheet.
- Appoint a manufacturing lead and quality owner.

### Days 6–10 — Build buyer-ready materials
- Prepare a concise capability profile.
- Prepare product specification and sample pack.
- Build the quotation calculator and cost approval process.
- Define sample, quality, delivery and complaint workflows.
- Complete conflict-of-interest disclosure and programme separation controls.

### Days 11–20 — Validate demand
- Conduct structured buyer discovery.
- Collect written buyer specifications, not just general interest.
- Identify the best-fit product/buyer combination.
- Check the buyer's supplier onboarding and payment process.
- Reject opportunities that exceed verified capacity or require unheld certifications.

### Days 21–30 — Select the controlled transaction
- Score the opportunity for demand, capability, margin, delivery, cash flow and risk.
- Prepare a fully costed quotation and sample plan.
- Obtain authorised commercial approval.
- Accept an order only when buyer terms, capacity, payment and working capital are acceptable.
- If no viable buyer brief emerges, extend discovery; do not manufacture speculative stock.

## 9. Success measures

| Measure | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Verified capability | Completed evidence pack |
| Qualified demand | Written buyer briefs and procurement requirements |
| Quotation quality | Approved cost model and submitted quote |
| Sample acceptance | Written buyer approval |
| Commercial conversion | Valid purchase order or contract |
| Delivery | Dispatch and buyer acceptance records |
| Quality | Inspection, defect, return and rework records |
| Financial performance | Reconciled actual cost, revenue and margin |
| Repeatability | Repeat order or documented buyer intent based on delivery |

No target quantity or revenue is set until current capacity, demand and working capital are known.

## 10. Go / no-go conditions

**Go** only when:
- buyer demand is documented;
- manufacturer capacity and specifications are verified;
- any required product certification is current and applicable;
- cost and margin are approved;
- working capital and payment terms are acceptable;
- quality and delivery controls are in place;
- conflict and related-party disclosures are recorded;
- the responsible legal entity approves the transaction.

**No-go / pause** when:
- buyer specifications are incomplete;
- capacity is unverified;
- required certifications are absent or out of scope;
- margins depend on unverified assumptions;
- the order requires unauthorised borrowing or guarantees;
- delivery dates cannot be met;
- conflicts cannot be managed transparently.

## 11. What this proves for AFRICA RISE

A successful controlled transaction would provide evidence that the initiative can:
- verify African manufacturing capability;
- connect manufacturers with real demand;
- improve procurement readiness;
- create measurable production and delivery outcomes;
- establish a repeatable model that can include additional manufacturers.

It would not, by itself, prove continental impact, national scale or job creation beyond verified records. Scale must follow repeat transactions and independently supportable results.
