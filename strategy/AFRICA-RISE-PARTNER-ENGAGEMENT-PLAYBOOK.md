# AFRICA RISE — Partner Engagement Playbook

## Purpose

Create a disciplined, evidence-led way to engage potential partners. This is an internal working playbook. It does not represent any organisation as a confirmed partner, sponsor, funder or endorser.

## Who we should engage

### CONNECT AFRICA
- licensed network operators and wholesale connectivity providers;
- MVNE/MVNO enablers and eSIM providers;
- fibre, fixed wireless and satellite providers;
- SME associations, business chambers and fleet operators;
- technical, cybersecurity and telecom regulatory specialists.

### BUILD AFRICA
- manufacturers and industrial service providers;
- procurement departments and anchor buyers;
- standards, testing and certification bodies;
- logistics, warehousing and export service providers;
- industrial development agencies and relevant business associations.

### EMPOWER AFRICA
- accredited education and training institutions;
- employers and sector skills bodies;
- universities, TVET colleges and research institutions;
- youth enterprise and mentorship organisations;
- workplace experience and placement partners.

### PROTECT AFRICA
- fleet operators and transport associations;
- device, automotive and telematics suppliers;
- cybersecurity and privacy specialists;
- emergency-response organisations and authorised service providers;
- insurers and road-safety organisations.

### TRADE AFRICA
- verified buyers, exporters, importers and manufacturers;
- chambers of commerce and trade associations;
- customs, freight and inspection specialists;
- trade finance and payment providers;
- standards and market-access institutions.

## Partner qualification

Score each prospect against evidence, not reputation or enthusiasm:
1. legal identity and beneficial ownership checks appropriate to the relationship;
2. demonstrated capability, references and delivery history;
3. alignment with a defined pilot and measurable customer need;
4. commercial terms, fees, payment conditions and liability;
5. regulatory standing and required licences/authorisations;
6. data access, privacy, cybersecurity and IP terms;
7. operational support, continuity and exit arrangements;
8. conflicts of interest, sanctions, anti-bribery and reputational risks.

Do not share confidential customer information, source code, security architecture or commercially sensitive data before appropriate confidentiality and access controls are in place.

## Engagement sequence

1. **Identify:** record organisation, jurisdiction, category, source and reason for relevance.
2. **Qualify:** verify identity, capability, authorisations and fit.
3. **Introduce:** send a concise, tailored explanation of AFRICA RISE and the specific programme opportunity.
4. **Discover:** establish the partner's needs, capability, constraints and expectations.
5. **Scope:** prepare a written concept note with responsibilities, deliverables, cost allocation and success measures.
6. **Review:** complete legal, financial, technical, security and governance checks.
7. **Agree:** obtain authorised written approval and signed terms where required.
8. **Pilot:** begin only after readiness gates are satisfied.
9. **Evaluate:** record evidence, performance, expenditure, issues and lessons.
10. **Renew or exit:** scale, renegotiate or close the relationship based on documented results.

## Standard introductory message

**Subject: Exploring a practical partnership with AFRICA RISE**

AFRICA RISE is an IZAKHONO GLOBAL initiative focused on African ownership, technology, enterprise, skills development and market access.

We are establishing a practical programme platform across five areas: Connect Africa, Build Africa, Empower Africa, Protect Africa and Trade Africa. Our approach is to begin with defined customer or community needs, accountable delivery, measurable outcomes and responsible partnerships.

We would welcome an exploratory discussion to understand your organisation's capabilities and priorities and to assess whether there is a specific pilot or area of mutual value worth developing.

At this stage, we are mapping capabilities and qualifying potential collaborators. This message is an invitation to explore possibilities and does not imply an existing partnership, funding commitment or endorsement.

If there is mutual interest, the next step would be a structured discovery discussion followed by a written concept note, due diligence and the appropriate approvals.

Kind regards,  
AFRICA RISE

## Meeting agenda (45 minutes)

1. Introductions and organisational capabilities — 5 minutes
2. Partner priorities and problem statement — 10 minutes
3. Relevant AFRICA RISE programme and proposed pilot — 10 minutes
4. Delivery model, roles and commercial considerations — 10 minutes
5. Risks, regulatory requirements and information needs — 5 minutes
6. Agreed next steps and accountable contacts — 5 minutes

## Required prospect register fields

- organisation legal name and trading name;
- country and operating jurisdictions;
- website and verified contact source;
- partner category and programme;
- products/services and evidence of capability;
- licences/accreditations where applicable;
- potential pilot and customer problem;
- proposed contribution and expected benefit;
- commercial model and cost exposure;
- due diligence status;
- data/IP/security considerations;
- relationship owner and next action;
- last contact and next review date;
- status: identified, qualifying, discovery, concept, due diligence, approved, active, declined or closed.

## Approval controls

- No announcement of a partnership based on a meeting, email exchange or verbal interest.
- No exclusivity, minimum purchase, financial guarantee or long-term commitment without explicit authorised approval.
- No public use of partner names or logos without written permission.
- No customer data or confidential technical information shared without lawful basis and appropriate controls.
- No grant or sponsorship representation until written award/contract terms are verified.
- Record declined and inactive prospects accurately; do not inflate partner counts.

## First engagement priorities

Begin with capability and demand discovery rather than broad mass outreach:
1. potential SME customers and authorised connectivity providers for Connect Africa;
2. genuine buyer specifications and verifiable manufacturing capacity for Build Africa;
3. employers and accredited providers for Empower Africa;
4. fleet operators and safety specialists for Protect Africa;
5. a defined buyer/supplier corridor and qualified counterparties for Trade Africa.

The first objective is to secure evidence of real problems and credible delivery pathways. The number of logos or letters of support is not a success measure.
