# AFRICA RISE — Initial Pilot Portfolio

## Purpose

This document turns the five founding programmes into small, testable pilot concepts. It does not approve expenditure, contracts, public launches or customer activations. Each pilot requires a named owner, validated demand, a costed plan, legal/compliance review and an explicit approval decision.

## Pilot selection rules

Select pilots that:
1. solve a clearly evidenced problem for a defined customer or community;
2. can be delivered with existing capabilities or credible partners;
3. have measurable outcomes within a controlled period;
4. have a plausible path to financial or operational sustainability;
5. do not depend on unapproved licences, unverified suppliers or untested safety claims.

## Pilot 1 — CONNECT AFRICA: SME connectivity and communications

**Concept:** A controlled business connectivity offer for a small cohort of South African SMEs, using an authorised wholesale/MVNO or reseller partner and the I-CONNECT customer, billing and service-management layer.

**Initial customer problem to validate:** SMEs deal with fragmented connectivity, communication channels, billing and support across multiple suppliers.

**IZAKHONO-owned work:** customer onboarding, account experience, plan catalogue, provisioning orchestration, service status, billing presentation, support workflow and audit.

**Partner-dependent work:** actual network access, SIM/eSIM issuance, voice/data carriage, numbering, emergency obligations and regulated services.

**Evidence required before pilot approval:**
- documented demand from prospective SME customers;
- written supplier offer, coverage/technology details, pricebook and service-level terms;
- confirmation of regulatory classification and any required authorisations;
- tested ordering, activation, cancellation, support and reconciliation in a partner sandbox;
- customer terms, privacy notices and complaint/escalation procedures;
- cost per customer and per unit of usage, margin model and working-capital requirement.

**Measures:** qualified prospects, paid pilot customers, successful activations, service availability, provisioning time, support resolution, churn, gross margin and cost-to-serve.

**Stop conditions:** no suitable authorised provider, unclear regulatory position, negative unit economics, unsafe data handling or repeated provisioning failures.

## Pilot 2 — BUILD AFRICA: manufacturing capability and procurement match

**Concept:** Build a verified supplier capability register and match a small number of African manufacturers to real buyer requirements. Start with categories where the founding network can demonstrate capability, quality and delivery evidence.

**Initial customer problem to validate:** buyers struggle to identify capable suppliers; manufacturers struggle to access qualified demand and procurement channels.

**IZAKHONO-owned work:** supplier capability profiles, product specifications, buyer discovery, quotation workflow, quality documentation and delivery tracking.

**Partner-dependent work:** independent product testing, certification, freight, customs, finance and buyer procurement approval.

**Evidence required before pilot approval:**
- verified supplier legal and operational details;
- capacity, lead times, samples and quality evidence;
- one or more real buyer specifications and procurement processes;
- landed-cost and margin analysis;
- clear payment, warranty, returns and dispute arrangements.

**Measures:** verified suppliers, qualified buyer briefs, quotations submitted, purchase orders won, on-time delivery, defect/return rate, local procurement value and repeat orders.

**Stop conditions:** inability to verify supplier capacity, no genuine buyer demand, unworkable quality requirements or unsustainable delivery economics.

## Pilot 3 — EMPOWER AFRICA: skills-to-opportunity pathway

**Concept:** A small employer-linked training cohort where curriculum, learner support and assessment are connected to a verified skills demand and a defined progression route.

**Initial customer problem to validate:** training can be disconnected from employer needs and practical opportunities.

**IZAKHONO-owned work:** employer discovery, programme coordination, learner journey, progress reporting and outcome tracking.

**Partner-dependent work:** accredited delivery, assessment, certification, placement and workplace experience must be performed by appropriately authorised institutions and employers.

**Evidence required before pilot approval:**
- employer-confirmed role or skills demand;
- verified accreditation and scope of the training provider;
- learner selection and safeguarding process;
- curriculum, assessment and support plan;
- realistic placement or progression commitments, without guaranteeing jobs.

**Measures:** enrolment, attendance, completion, demonstrated competencies, employer satisfaction, interviews, placements and six-month progression where consent and follow-up permit.

**Stop conditions:** accreditation mismatch, no employer demand, inadequate learner support or misleading employment claims.

## Pilot 4 — PROTECT AFRICA: fleet safety and device-trust workflow

**Concept:** A controlled fleet pilot using existing FORTRESS and IZAKHONO NAV development to test device identity, authorised access, safety-event workflows and evidence-backed escalation.

**Initial customer problem to validate:** fleet operators need reliable device accountability, driver safety processes and clear incident records.

**IZAKHONO-owned work:** device registration, identity/session checks, role access, audit trail, safety-event interface and incident workflow.

**Partner-dependent work:** emergency dispatch, roadside response, telecom delivery, tracking hardware, insurance and law-enforcement coordination require appropriate providers and agreements.

**Evidence required before pilot approval:**
- fleet operator consent and a defined test cohort;
- threat model, privacy impact assessment and retention rules;
- supported devices and tested connectivity assumptions;
- documented human escalation and incident-handling procedures;
- clear disclosure of limitations and no claim of guaranteed rescue or response;
- security testing and incident rollback plan.

**Measures:** device enrolment success, access-control test results, event capture completeness, alert delivery evidence, human acknowledgement time, false-alert rate and driver feedback.

**Stop conditions:** unresolved privacy/security risk, misleading emergency capability, unsafe reliance on untested features or lack of operator consent.

## Pilot 5 — TRADE AFRICA: verified buyer–supplier transaction

**Concept:** Facilitate a limited number of genuine buyer–supplier introductions and transactions in a selected product/service category and corridor.

**Initial customer problem to validate:** smaller businesses struggle to find credible counterparties and navigate cross-border procurement.

**IZAKHONO-owned work:** business verification workflow, supplier catalogue, buyer requirements, introduction tracking, quote exchange and transaction outcome reporting.

**Partner-dependent work:** payments, trade finance, export documentation, customs, logistics, inspection and dispute resolution require qualified service providers.

**Evidence required before pilot approval:**
- selected product category and corridor;
- verified buyers and suppliers;
- product standards, origin and export/import requirements;
- written quotations, delivery terms and payment protections;
- sanctions, fraud, anti-bribery and counterparty screening appropriate to the transaction;
- clear responsibility for disputes, delivery and refunds.

**Measures:** verified counterparties, qualified introductions, quotations, completed transactions, transaction value, delivery performance and repeat trade.

**Stop conditions:** unverifiable counterparties, unclear import/export legality, unacceptable payment risk or no credible fulfilment route.

## Standard pilot approval pack

No pilot may move into live delivery until the following pack is complete:

| Requirement | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Accountable owner | Named lead and delegated authority |
| Demand | Customer/beneficiary research and documented interest |
| Scope | Deliverables, exclusions, timeline and cohort |
| Budget | Itemised costs, funding source, cash flow and approvals |
| Commercial model | Price, cost-to-serve, margin or sustainability case |
| Legal/compliance | Jurisdiction-specific review and required permissions |
| Partner readiness | Signed terms where required; technical test evidence |
| Security/privacy | Data map, access controls, retention and incident plan |
| Operations | Support, escalation, continuity and rollback |
| Measurement | Baseline, targets, evidence source and reporting cadence |

## Portfolio sequencing recommendation

Build the foundations in parallel, but do not attempt five full-scale launches simultaneously.

1. **Start with capability discovery:** manufacturing, education, SME connectivity and trade demand can be researched immediately.
2. **Advance product foundations:** secure I-CONNECT tenant isolation and provider qualification; continue FORTRESS/NAV testing within explicit safety limits.
3. **Select the first live pilot:** choose the one with the strongest verified demand, partner readiness, compliance path and unit economics.
4. **Use pilot evidence to scale:** expand only after customer acceptance and operating performance are demonstrated.

## Financial discipline

This portfolio intentionally contains no invented budget figures. Each pilot budget must be prepared from current quotations, staff capacity, travel, equipment, technology, compliance, insurance, tax and working-capital requirements. Grant funding, sponsorship and commercial revenue must be separately tracked and used according to their terms.
