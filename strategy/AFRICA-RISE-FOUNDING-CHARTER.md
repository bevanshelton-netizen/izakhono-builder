# AFRICA RISE — Founding Charter

**Status:** Founding working charter for governance and planning. It is not a registered constitution, shareholder agreement, trust deed, funding contract or public-sector mandate.

## 1. Founding purpose

AFRICA RISE exists to advance African ownership, productive capacity, technology, skills, enterprise and market access through practical programmes that are governed, measurable and financially responsible.

It begins with South Africa as the initial operating market and is designed to build partnerships and capability across African markets over time. Each country and sector must be approached according to its own laws, institutions, communities and commercial realities.

## 2. Founding proposition

Africa must participate in shaping, owning and operating the systems that create its future. AFRICA RISE will work to:
- expand African-owned enterprise and intellectual property;
- strengthen manufacturing and industrial capability;
- connect skills development to verified opportunity;
- develop trusted, accessible communications and technology;
- improve access to markets, procurement and trade;
- build partnerships that transfer capability and produce measurable value.

## 3. Relationship to IZAKHONO

AFRICA RISE is currently framed as an initiative of IZAKHONO GLOBAL within the IZAKHONO ecosystem. The exact legal entity responsible for contracting, holding assets, receiving funds, employing staff and carrying programme liabilities must be confirmed before any live activity requiring those functions.

IZAKHONO may provide technology, intellectual property, programme management, business services or delivery capacity under documented internal arrangements. No separate legal personality is implied by the initiative name.

## 4. Five founding programmes

### Connect Africa
Develop inclusive communications and connectivity services through software, qualified network partners and selectively owned infrastructure. Regulated network and communications services must not be activated until the applicable South African and other jurisdictional requirements are confirmed and satisfied.

### Build Africa
Strengthen manufacturing, industrial capability, local procurement, technology development and African-owned intellectual property. Supplier capacity, product standards and buyer demand must be verified.

### Empower Africa
Connect education and technical skills to employer demand, enterprise development and practical progression. Accredited training, assessment and certification must be delivered only by appropriately authorised institutions within their approved scope.

### Protect Africa
Advance digital trust, device security, safer mobility and responsible emergency communications. Security and safety claims must match independently tested capability. No product may promise guaranteed rescue, emergency response or protection unless the service is genuinely provided under verified arrangements.

### Trade Africa
Connect credible African producers, service providers, buyers and markets. Counterparty verification, product standards, payment risk, customs, sanctions and trade compliance must be addressed for each transaction and corridor.

## 5. Operating principles

1. **African ownership:** protect local capability, IP and productive value.
2. **Evidence before claims:** distinguish ideas, prototypes, pilots and operational services.
3. **Commercial discipline:** understand costs, margins, cash flow and sustainability.
4. **Partnership with control:** collaborate without casually surrendering strategic IP, customer relationships or decision rights.
5. **Integrity:** prevent bribery, fraud, conflicts of interest and misleading public representations.
6. **Inclusion and dignity:** design with affected communities and avoid exploitative practices.
7. **Security and privacy:** minimise data, restrict access and respect applicable data-protection laws.
8. **Environmental and social responsibility:** assess material impacts and avoid preventable harm.
9. **Local compliance:** obtain jurisdiction-specific advice and approvals before regulated activity.
10. **Accountability:** record decisions, expenditure, outcomes, risks and corrective action.

## 6. Governance model to establish

The following is a proposed operating model, not a claim that these bodies or appointments already exist:

- **Sponsor / Founding Executive:** sets strategic direction and approves reserved matters.
- **Programme Steering Committee:** coordinates programme priorities, resolves cross-programme dependencies and reviews performance.
- **Programme Leads:** accountable for scope, budget, delivery, risk, partners and outcomes in their programme.
- **Finance and Risk Control:** reviews budgets, funding restrictions, procurement, financial reporting and material risks.
- **Technology and Security Authority:** reviews architecture, privacy, cybersecurity, data access, production readiness and incident response.
- **Independent advisers:** provide specialist legal, regulatory, audit, technical, education, trade and community advice where required.

Appointments, delegated powers, quorum, voting, conflicts, reporting and remuneration must be formally documented by the responsible legal entity.

## 7. Reserved decisions

The following require documented approval by the authorised legal entity or its delegated decision-maker:
- borrowing, guarantees, equity issuance or material capital commitments;
- contracts with exclusivity, minimum purchase, long duration or material liability;
- public funding applications, grant acceptance and restricted-fund commitments;
- public-sector agreements or representations of government endorsement;
- regulated service launches and use of licences or spectrum;
- material IP transfers, licences, source-code disclosure or customer-data sharing;
- appointment or removal of senior programme leadership;
- public announcements of partnerships, funding, customer numbers or operational capability;
- material changes to mission, legal structure or ownership.

## 8. Financial controls

- Maintain separate records for commercial revenue, grants, sponsorships, donations and restricted funding.
- Approve an itemised budget and funding source before committing expenditure.
- Use documented procurement and conflict-of-interest declarations.
- Require evidence of delivery, invoices and approvals before payment.
- Reconcile bank, payment-provider and accounting records regularly.
- Prohibit personal use of programme funds and undisclosed related-party transactions.
- Report budget, actuals, commitments, cash flow, variances and risks to the authorised governance body.
- Do not publish budget or funding totals unless reconciled and approved.

## 9. Performance and reporting

Each programme must report:
- objectives, scope and delivery status;
- customers, beneficiaries and stakeholder feedback;
- outputs and independently supportable outcomes;
- expenditure, funding restrictions and forecast;
- partner and supplier status;
- regulatory, privacy, security and operational risks;
- incidents, complaints and corrective actions;
- next-period priorities and decisions required.

Quarterly reporting is the intended steady-state cadence. More frequent reporting may be required during pilots.

## 10. Public communications

AFRICA RISE may describe its mission, proposed programmes and work in preparation. It must not claim:
- partners, funders, sponsors or government support without written evidence and permission;
- jobs, training placements, customer numbers, revenue, coverage or impact without verified records;
- licences, accreditation or regulatory approval that have not been confirmed;
- a pilot or service is live when it is only a design, prototype or test.

All public enquiries must be routed through a monitored channel with an approved privacy notice before personal information is collected.

## 11. Charter adoption and legal review

This charter is an operating draft. Before formal adoption, the responsible legal entity must review it for consistency with its constitutional documents, ownership, statutory duties, tax position, funding rules and applicable law. Adoption must be recorded in an authorised resolution.

## 12. First-year strategic test

AFRICA RISE should be judged by the quality and durability of what it helps build: productive African businesses, useful technology, credible skills pathways, trusted partnerships, completed trade and measurable community and economic value — not by announcements or the number of programmes bearing its name.
