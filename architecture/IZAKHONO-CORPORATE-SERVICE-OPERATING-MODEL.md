# IZAKHONO Corporate Service & Commercial Operating Model

**Status:** Canonical corporate capability and service-delivery model  
**Brand:** IZAKHONO — Intelligent Technology. Engineered Infrastructure.  
**Positioning:** We engineer the technology businesses depend on.  
**Supporting line:** Built in Africa. Engineered for the world.

This document absorbs proven digital-transformation service patterns into IZAKHONO's own operating system. It is an IZAKHONO-authored model, not a reproduction of another provider's branding, copy, design, case studies, credentials, partnerships or claims. External market references are inputs for capability design only; IZAKHONO must independently substantiate every public claim.

## 1. One integrated company

IZAKHONO operates as one integrated technology engineering and infrastructure company. Artificial intelligence, software, infrastructure, security, automation, data and managed services are coordinated practices in one customer lifecycle—not disconnected brands or unrelated service silos.

The company promise is to assess, architect, build, integrate, secure, deploy, manage and continuously improve the technology businesses depend on.

## 3. End-to-end customer lifecycle

Every engagement is mapped to the same delivery lifecycle:

1. **Assess** — understand business outcomes, users, existing systems, constraints, risks and baseline performance.
2. **Architect** — define target architecture, scope, delivery plan, dependencies, security controls, costs and acceptance criteria.
3. **Build** — engineer the agreed application, AI capability, infrastructure or automation.
4. **Integrate** — connect customer systems, data, identity, channels and third-party services through controlled interfaces.
5. **Secure** — apply threat modelling, access controls, privacy, testing, audit and operational safeguards.
6. **Deploy** — release only through authorised environments and verified deployment gates; preserve rollback and evidence.
7. **Manage** — provide the contracted support, monitoring, maintenance and service management.
8. **Improve** — review outcomes, cost, performance, risk and adoption; agree the next optimisation cycle.

Each project must have an accountable owner, scope, change control, risk register, acceptance criteria, evidence record and customer handover.

## 4. Service delivery and commercial engagement models

IZAKHONO supports four engagement shapes. They are commercial options, not claims that every practice is currently staffed or operational at production scale.

- **Assessment and advisory:** fixed-scope discovery, audits, readiness reviews, architecture and roadmaps.
- **Project delivery:** milestone-based implementation with defined deliverables, dependencies and acceptance.
- **Managed services:** recurring support, monitoring, maintenance, infrastructure or security operations against an agreed service description and measurable service levels.
- **Dedicated expertise / team augmentation:** time-bound or retained engineering capacity with named roles, governance and delivery accountability.

Consulting can be a standalone engagement or the first phase of a larger project. Customer proposals must identify the delivery model, assumptions, exclusions, responsibilities, fees, payment milestones, change process and support boundary.

## 5. Revenue architecture and initial offers

Revenue should be earned through clearly scoped value, not artificial AI credits or unverified infrastructure promises.

### Entry offers
- **Technology & AI Opportunity Assessment:** business and technology discovery, prioritised opportunities, risk review and practical roadmap.
- **Infrastructure & Resilience Review:** architecture, deployment, backup, recovery, cost and operational-risk assessment.
- **Application & Integration Review:** application health, technical debt, integration map, security considerations and modernisation plan.
- **Security Posture Review:** scoped control review and prioritised remediation plan; not represented as a certification or penetration test unless that is the contracted and qualified service.

Each paid assessment requires an approved scope, deliverables, turnaround, fee, payment terms and named delivery owner before it is marketed as a purchasable offer. Do not invent prices or guaranteed turnaround times.

### Project revenue
AI implementation; software and platform development; integration; cloud/owned-infrastructure migration; application modernisation; security improvement; workflow automation; data and analytics implementation.

### Recurring revenue
Managed infrastructure; hosting only where production readiness is verified; application maintenance; monitoring; managed IT; security operations; support retainers; optimisation retainers.

### Product and subscription revenue
Subscriptions, licences and transaction-based product fees only where product capability, tariff disclosure, customer terms, billing state and payment reconciliation are implemented and verified. I-PAY remains governed by its own release and payment-provider readiness gates.

### Enterprise revenue
Transformation programmes; multi-phase engineering engagements; dedicated teams; enterprise support and long-term managed contracts.

## 6. Sales-to-delivery operating system

The customer operating flow is:

**Discover → Qualify → Assess → Propose → Contract → Onboard → Deliver → Accept → Support → Renew / Expand**

Minimum system records:
- account and authorised customer contacts;
- lead source, business need, qualification and next action;
- opportunity value and stage (value is never fabricated);
- service/practice, engagement model and proposed scope;
- proposal, contract, statement of work and approval history;
- project owner, delivery team, milestones, dependencies and risks;
- customer requirements, acceptance evidence and handover;
- invoice, payment status, reconciliation and outstanding balance;
- support plan, incidents, service reviews and renewal dates.

The CRM and service-management layer must maintain tenant and customer boundaries. Commercial records, project artefacts, audit events and payment state must be access-controlled and traceable. No customer data may be exposed across tenants.

## 7. Billing and payment-provider architecture

IZAKHONO owns its customer, product, plan, quote, invoice, subscription and payment-ledger records. Payment providers are adapters, not the source of truth for the entire commercial relationship.

- **Primary South African collection route:** iKhokha, subject to verified merchant API access, current documentation, secure credential provisioning, webhook/signature verification and reconciliation tests.
- **Enterprise and project fallback:** approved invoice and EFT process with recorded allocation and reconciliation.
- **International provider:** Stripe may be considered as an optional future adapter, never a prerequisite for core development or South African launch.

Never store payment secrets in source control or ask customers to send credentials in ordinary chat. A payment is successful only after authoritative provider confirmation and internal reconciliation. Do not claim a live payment integration until sandbox and production acceptance evidence exists.

## 8. Capability and public-claim governance

Every service, product, integration and operational promise must carry an internal readiness state:

- **AVAILABLE** — implemented, staffed, supportable and evidenced for the stated scope.
- **LIMITED / PILOT** — usable only within explicit constraints, pilot scope and named acceptance criteria.
- **IN DEVELOPMENT** — active engineering; not sold as a generally available production service.
- **PLANNED** — approved roadmap intent; not represented as an existing capability.

Public pages and proposals must be generated from or reconciled against this register. Do not publish fabricated clients, case studies, outcomes, testimonials, partner badges, certifications, service-level agreements, uptime figures, prices or deployment status. Do not imply endorsement, partnership or affiliation with any external benchmark provider.

## 9. Architecture and security invariants

- Preserve existing product names and component boundaries: HOST, Runtime Fabric, FORTRESS, THOR, PROVISIONER, BUILDER, NODE identities and I-PAY.
- NODE01 is software; the execution environment is separate, replaceable and must be verified.
- Production deployment requires authorised execution, runtime registration, tenant/environment isolation, security checks, audit integrity, backup/restore evidence, DNS/TLS verification and end-to-end acceptance.
- Deployment state and audit evidence must be committed consistently; idempotency must be correctly scoped; authorisation must be enforced at tenant, application and environment boundaries.
- Do not bypass existing CI, security or release gates to make a service appear live.
- Keep the owned-hosting architecture as the intended production path; external hosting remains a reversible fallback where explicitly approved.

## 10. Operating cadence and measures

Review the business and delivery system on a recurring cadence using evidence-backed measures:
- qualified leads, conversion and sales-cycle duration;
- assessment-to-project conversion;
- contracted revenue, collected revenue and overdue invoices;
- recurring revenue, renewal and churn;
- delivery predictability, milestone acceptance and change requests;
- escaped defects, incident volume and time to restore;
- service availability only where measured and contractually relevant;
- security findings, remediation age and audit completeness;
- customer adoption, satisfaction and realised business outcomes.

Do not report a metric without a defined source, calculation, period and accountable owner.
