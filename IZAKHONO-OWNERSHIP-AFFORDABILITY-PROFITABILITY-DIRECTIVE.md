# IZAKHONO OWNERSHIP, AFFORDABILITY & PROFITABILITY DIRECTIVE

**Issued by:** IZAKHONO AFRICA (PTY) LTD  
**Status:** Standing company-wide product, infrastructure and commercial directive  
**Applies to:** All IZAKHONO products, platforms, infrastructure, AI workers, engineering teams and commercial operations

## 1. Strategic objective

IZAKHONO will build and operate an owned-first technology ecosystem that is affordable to customers, commercially sustainable, independently operable and capable of scaling across African and international markets.

The company will not allow an external platform, AI provider, hosting company, payment provider or infrastructure supplier to become an irreplaceable point of control over its products, customer relationships, source code, data or ability to trade.

Ownership is pursued to improve control, resilience, cost efficiency and long-term profitability—not to pretend that infrastructure has no operating cost.

## 2. Ownership and exitability

Every product must document:
- the IZAKHONO-owned source of truth for code, configuration, data and product specifications;
- the primary owned execution and hosting route;
- all external dependencies, their purpose, cost, permissions and renewal terms;
- a tested or testable export, migration, replacement or exit path;
- backup, restore, disaster recovery and service continuity arrangements;
- customer data portability and secure deletion procedures.

External services may be used where they are commercially or technically justified. They must remain replaceable. Do not introduce an external dependency as the sole route for a critical capability without an explicit owner-approved exception and a documented exit plan.

## 3. Credits-free customer experience

IZAKHONO products must not impose artificial AI credits, prompt credits, build credits or pay-per-action tokens on customers as a default commercial mechanism.

Internally, the company must measure actual resource consumption, including compute, model inference, storage, bandwidth, payment processing, support and third-party service costs. Capacity controls, abuse prevention, fair-use limits and security throttles remain permitted when transparently described and operationally justified.

The native IZAKHONO Builder is the built-in application-building provider. Hercules is not part of the execution path.

## 4. Mass adoption and affordability

The commercial strategy is to reduce the barrier to entry, earn customer trust, grow recurring customer relationships and expand account value through useful products and bundles.

Pricing principles:
- affordable entry tiers for individuals, entrepreneurs and small businesses;
- clear, predictable monthly and annual pricing;
- low-friction onboarding and transparent setup charges;
- bundles that reward adoption across the IZAKHONO ecosystem;
- upgrade paths as customer needs and usage grow;
- enterprise, institutional, licensing and white-label pricing where value and support obligations justify it;
- no hidden fees or misleading "unlimited" claims.

Low pricing is a strategic option, not permission to sell below sustainable cost indefinitely. Promotions and loss-leading offers must have a defined acquisition budget, duration, eligibility, conversion hypothesis and stop/review threshold.

## 5. Mandatory unit economics

Before public launch and at each material pricing change, every product owner must maintain a costed unit-economics model containing, where applicable:

- list price, discounts, taxes and net recognised revenue;
- payment processing and collection costs;
- compute, model inference, storage, bandwidth and egress;
- external licences, APIs and infrastructure;
- onboarding, support, moderation and maintenance;
- refunds, chargebacks, fraud and bad debt;
- customer acquisition cost and channel cost;
- churn, retention, cohort revenue and lifetime value assumptions;
- gross profit, gross margin and contribution margin;
- break-even customer count and capacity thresholds.

Use actual measured costs when available. Clearly label assumptions and forecasts. Do not present illustrative scenarios as actual performance.

Core calculations:

Revenue per customer = collected recurring revenue attributable to the customer, excluding pass-through taxes where appropriate.

Contribution per customer = net revenue minus variable costs directly attributable to serving and supporting that customer.

Gross margin = (net revenue minus cost of service) / net revenue.

Break-even customer count = fixed operating costs / average contribution per customer, provided average contribution is positive.

Pricing decisions must consider customer affordability, competitive conditions, service quality, capacity and sustainable contribution—not revenue volume alone.

## 6. Portfolio-wide commercial control

J.A.R.V.I.S. and the IZAKHONO control plane should progressively provide:
- product-level recurring and one-off revenue;
- active, trial, overdue and churned customers;
- customer acquisition and retention;
- actual cost per customer, workload and product;
- infrastructure utilisation and cost per unit of capacity;
- gross and contribution margins;
- bundle adoption and expansion revenue;
- supplier concentration and dependency exposure;
- forecast versus actual break-even and cash requirements.

Metrics must be sourced from authorised, reconciled systems. Missing or stale values must be marked unavailable; never fabricate live financial figures.

## 7. Infrastructure economics

The owned infrastructure programme must measure total cost of ownership, not merely compare supplier invoices. Include hardware or compute, power, connectivity, colocation, cooling, backups, security, engineering, monitoring, replacement cycles, resilience and spare capacity.

Scale capacity in measured stages. Use workload scheduling, caching, efficient model routing, storage lifecycle policies, observability and resource isolation to lower cost per customer. Do not purchase or provision capacity solely on speculative customer counts.

A Software NODE or physical node is considered production-eligible only after health, capacity, persistent storage, monitoring, backup/restore, security and external reachability requirements have been verified.

## 8. Payment and customer trust

Payment providers must be replaceable through a documented adapter and reconciliation interface. Prefer the company-approved South African payment route where it is operationally suitable, but do not hard-code a single provider into business logic.

Customer subscriptions must have clear terms, cancellation, refund handling, receipts, privacy protections and support routes. No pricing or billing change may silently alter existing customer commitments.

## 9. Portfolio execution requirements

For every product, the delivery checklist must include:
1. Product scope and target customer.
2. Owned-first architecture and dependency register.
3. Security, privacy and customer-data controls.
4. Working product and complete critical customer journeys.
5. Measured service capacity and cost model.
6. Pricing tiers, bundles and commercial terms.
7. Billing, payment reconciliation and cancellation flows where applicable.
8. Analytics for acquisition, activation, retention and profitability.
9. Automated tests, operational monitoring and recovery procedures.
10. Release evidence, documentation and customer handover.

The continuous delivery cycle remains: BUILD → EXECUTE → TEST → FIX → VERIFY → PACKAGE → DEPLOY (only when authorised and release gates pass) → VERIFY RELEASE.

## 10. Decision rights and safeguards

Workers may independently research, design, implement, test, repair and package ordinary reversible work. They must not make unauthorised financial commitments, purchase infrastructure, change production billing, alter customer contracts, move or expose customer data, rotate secrets, or perform irreversible production actions.

Escalate only a specific decision that genuinely requires the owner's authority. Continue independent work while that decision is pending.

## 11. Company principle

**OWN THE TECHNOLOGY. CONTROL THE COST. MAKE THE PRODUCT AFFORDABLE. EARN TRUST. GROW THE CUSTOMER BASE. PROTECT THE MARGIN.**

IZAKHONO will compete through ownership, efficiency, accessibility, service quality, ecosystem value and recurring customer relationships—not artificial credits, vendor lock-in or unsustainable pricing.
